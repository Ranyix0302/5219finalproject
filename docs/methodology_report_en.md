# Methodology C (Bingsheng)

Implementation snapshot: 17 October 2026; handover revision: 19 October 2026. The following describes the implemented GIS-MCDA component. Conversation-to-preference validation and website integration remain separate tasks; no user study or field validation is claimed.

## 4.1 GIS-based Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis

### 4.1.1 Analytical Units and Indicator Matrix

The candidate universe consists of residential building records in the supplied datasets, rather than all URA-planned residential land or currently available rentals. Of 96,977 input records, 214 records in 107 conflicting cross-source postcode groups were held out pending reconciliation, leaving 96,763 candidate records in 241 official Subzones. A postcode alone is therefore not assumed to establish unique building identity. Distances and buffers are calculated in Singapore SVY21 (EPSG:3414); WGS84 is used for API requests and map exchange.

The baseline, sample A, selects one actual residential point per occupied cell × Subzone intersection of a fixed 400 m grid: the residence nearest the cell centre, with stable-ID tie breaking. A cell crossing Subzone boundaries can therefore contribute several points. This yields 2,176 planned locations. These points represent sampled residential locations; they are not a population-weighted or building-exhaustive survey. Seven indicators are used, in this fixed order: (1) campus itinerary duration in minutes; (2) nearest MRT/LRT exit straight-line distance in metres; (3) distinct bus stops within 800 m; (4) supermarket sites within 800 m; (5) nearest public-library straight-line distance; (6) clinic sites within 1,000 m; and (7) straight-line proximity to retained park polygons. The first, second, fifth and seventh are costs; the counts are benefits. For a point p, nearest distance is min over facility geometries g of distance(p,g), and supply count is the sum of indicators that distance(p,g) is at most the stated radius. Park distance is zero inside a retained polygon. These are proximity or supply proxies, not measured walking accessibility, service capacity, opening hours, bus frequency or park-entry access. Hawker centres, gyms and rental prices are not scored in this version.

Facility preprocessing preserves source identity and uses explicit site/address rules. Four unopened Marina South station-exit records were excluded; this does not certify every other exit. Supermarket and clinic licences are consolidated to service sites where the address rules support it, while unresolved unit addresses are retained and flagged. Seven source polygons carrying the N_RESERVE label are excluded provisionally; they must not be described as seven legally designated nature reserves. Park polygon distance avoids unreliable centroid placement but does not account for entrances or barriers. [TO DISCUSS: confirm park categories, unresolved service addresses, candidate completeness and the behavioural justification for 800/1,000 m thresholds.]

The first release uses NUS AS8 as a reviewed ground-arrival proxy and requests OneMap TRANSIT routes for 19 October 2026 at 08:00 (Asia/Singapore). The frozen campus indicator is the first returned itinerary's duration divided by 60. This definition can include a walking-only itinerary; it does not guarantee boarding public transport. Desktop checks also found walking legs above the requested maxWalkDistance=1,000 m despite walkLimitExceeded=false, and initial departure delays that are absent from itinerary duration. For example, one reviewed itinerary reports 110.77 minutes, whereas 08:00-to-arrival is 123.52 minutes. Consequently the current indicator must not be described as an enforced walking limit or total elapsed time from the requested departure. Six NoRoute responses are treated as unknown, not zero or evidence of real-world inaccessibility. Twenty valid routes plus one unavailable case received AI-assisted desktop checks using the same provider; this is neither independent-provider validation nor field verification. ### 4.1.2 Indicator Normalization

For indicator j, L_j and U_j are its 5th and 95th percentiles among sample A's finite raw observations. The clipped value is x*_ij = min(U_j, max(L_j, x_ij)). Benefit scores are z_ij = (x*_ij - L_j)/(U_j - L_j); cost scores are z_ij = (U_j - x*_ij)/(U_j - L_j). Thus all scores range from zero to one, with higher values preferable. For example, supermarket bounds of 0 and 7 give three sites a score of 3/7, approximately 0.429. The bounds are frozen across preference, cap and spatial comparisons, including supplementary sample B; they are not refitted to each scenario. Missing values remain missing. A constant or otherwise invalid normalization range triggers review rather than silent deletion or imputation. Standardization aligns units but cannot establish conceptual validity or remove indicator redundancy.

### 4.1.3 Preference Weighting and Hard Constraints

Nonnegative importance inputs a_j are converted to weights w_j = a_j / sum(a_j), requiring a positive total. This is direct importance weighting, not AHP or empirically estimated student preferences. In indicator order above, the balanced baseline is (0.25, 0.15, 0.10, 0.15, 0.10, 0.10, 0.15). Three illustrative alternatives are campus-first (0.40, 0.20, 0.10, 0.10, 0.05, 0.05, 0.10), transit-first (0.15, 0.35, 0.25, 0.10, 0.05, 0.05, 0.05), and lifestyle (0.10, 0.10, 0.10, 0.20, 0.15, 0.15, 0.20). All seven measurements remain required even if one weight is zero.

The default imposes no commute cap. Separate 40- and 60-minute caps are applied to raw campus minutes before recommendation; a high amenity score cannot compensate for exceeding a chosen cap. For area g with N_g planned locations, V_g valid locations and F_g locations verified to satisfy every hard constraint, eligibility requires V_g/N_g >= 0.90 and F_g/N_g >= 0.50. Unknown locations remain in the planned denominator. These thresholds are explicit decision rules, not literature-derived optima. Areas with fewer than three valid points are prominently flagged. ### 4.1.4 Weighted Scoring and Spatial Aggregation

A valid point that satisfies the active hard constraints receives S_i = sum_j(w_j z_ij), and its contribution from indicator j is c_ij = w_j z_ij. An eligible area's score is the arithmetic mean of S_i over its feasible points; reported area contributions are means over the same points. Each sampled location has equal weight. The score therefore describes the planned sample and is not a household-weighted measure of all residents or every building. Planned, valid and feasible counts accompany every result. Full-precision scores determine ordering; ties use lower mean raw commute, then lower mean rail distance, then the stable area identifier.

### 4.1.5 Top-5 Recommendation and Explainability

The result bundle returns up to five eligible Subzones with area scores, seven mean contributions, mean commute/rail distance, coverage and low-support warnings. Point-level raw and normalized indicators are provided separately and linked through location and unit identifiers; they are not all columns in the Top-5 table. The language model may explain these returned values but must not invent scores, facilities or availability. In the frozen balanced/no-cap result, 239 of 241 Subzones are eligible; four of the Top 5 have only one or two valid locations. This is a material limitation of the recommendation evidence, even where rank sensitivity is low. ## 4.2 Sensitivity and Robustness Analysis

### 4.2.1 Preference Scenario Analysis

The four weight vectors are compared on A/Subzone without a commute cap, holding data, definitions, eligibility rules and normalization fixed. Their Top-5 Jaccard values range from 0.429 to 0.667, and Spearman rank correlations over 239 common eligible Subzones range from 0.788 to 0.944. This indicates meaningful preference dependence. The scenarios are illustrative tests, not survey findings. Cap and eligibility-threshold tests are reported separately to avoid mixing the effects of weights and filtering.

### 4.2.2 Monte Carlo Simulation

Using random seed 42, 1,000 joint weight vectors are drawn from Dirichlet(50w_balanced). All drawn weights are nonnegative and sum to one. The concentration 50 controls dispersion around the baseline and is a modelling choice, not a fitted measure of population uncertainty. Each draw recomputes all area scores and ranks on the unchanged A/no-cap specification. We report Top-5 selection frequency, median rank and 5th/95th percentile ranks. For example, BOAT QUAY enters the Top 5 in 602/1,000 draws: 60.2% is conditional on this weight distribution, not a probability that the area is objectively best or suitable for a particular student. The experiment varies weights only, leaving measurement error, route schedules, sample design and facility omissions untested.

### 4.2.3 One-at-a-Time Weight Sensitivity

Each indicator weight is set in turn to t = 0.05, 0.10, ..., 0.60. Every other weight k becomes w'_k = w_k(1-t)/(1-w_j), preserving their relative proportions and a unit total. Seven indicators and 12 targets produce 84 runs. Comparisons between adjacent targets produce 77 intervals, 17 of which change Top-5 membership. A change between 0.25 and 0.30 locates a switch within that tested interval; it is not an exact critical weight. This analysis identifies influential preferences but does not prove that an indicator definition is appropriate.

### 4.2.4 Spatial Scale and Aggregation Sensitivity

Within each Subzone, candidate points connected through pairwise distances of at most 150 m form an analysis cluster. Chaining is allowed, so clusters can extend well beyond 150 m and do not establish real housing-estate boundaries. There are 384 clusters, including 44 singletons. Sample B adds one real residential point to each cluster missed by A: 20 additions, for 2,196 planned locations. Three comparisons use balanced weights, a 60-minute cap and A's frozen normalization: A/Subzone (2,176 points), B/Subzone (2,196), and B/cluster (2,196). The first pair isolates the supplementary-sampling effect; the second holds locations fixed while changing aggregation units. It does not test 250/500 m grids, which remain unimplemented.

There are 167 eligible Subzones in both A and B and 276 eligible B clusters. A/B Subzone Top-5 membership is identical, with rank correlation approximately 0.99948. For B/Subzone versus B/cluster, area identifiers are incomparable; overlap is evaluated on common planned locations and on the full candidate-building membership associated with selected units. Location Jaccard is 4/14 = 0.286, and building-record Jaccard is 16/65 = 0.246. These are overlaps of selected geographic footprints with different unit sizes, not direct evidence that clusters are better or that the model is unstable. ### 4.2.5 Ranking Stability Evaluation

For comparable units, Top-5 Jaccard is |T_A intersect T_B|/|T_A union T_B|, while Spearman correlation measures ordering among common eligible units. Scores, eligibility, membership and geographic overlap are interpreted separately. Forty- and sixty-minute caps leave 51 and 167 eligible Subzones, respectively; at a fixed 60-minute cap, feasible-share thresholds of 25%, 50% and 75% leave 178, 167 and 152 eligible Subzones, with the same Top 5. Stability under these tested settings is conditional evidence of robustness. It neither validates conceptual choices nor remedies sparse samples, correlated indicators, stale or incomplete facilities, or the observed OneMap timing and walking issues. The deliverable is an auditable decision-support prototype, not a certified rental recommendation service.
